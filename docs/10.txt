# Document 10: Risk Engine

## Project Name

# Atlas

**Version:** MVP 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Risk Engine is responsible for producing transparent, explainable, and evidence-backed risk assessments.

Its role is **not** to determine whether a wallet is "good" or "bad."

Instead, it measures the likelihood that an observed behavior deserves further investigation.

Every risk score generated by Atlas must be reproducible, explainable, and supported by evidence.

The Risk Engine exists to help investigators prioritize their attention, not replace their judgment.

---

# Core Philosophy

Risk is a probability, not a verdict.

Atlas should never label a wallet as malicious solely because of a high numerical score.

Instead, Atlas should explain:

* why the score exists
* what evidence supports it
* what uncertainty remains
* what additional evidence could change the assessment

Transparency is more important than the score itself.

---

# Responsibilities

The Risk Engine is responsible for:

* calculating risk scores
* calculating confidence scores
* evaluating behavioral indicators
* combining signals from multiple agents
* generating explainable reasoning
* detecting changes in risk over time
* producing investigation priorities
* monitoring historical risk evolution

The Risk Engine **does not** investigate wallets or store memory.

It evaluates intelligence already collected by the platform.

# Risk Categories

Atlas separates different forms of risk.

## Financial Risk

Indicators:

* laundering
* suspicious fund movement
* mixer interaction

---

## Behavioral Risk

Indicators:

* unusual transaction sequences
* repeated wallet creation
* coordinated timing

---

## Protocol Risk

Indicators:

* exploited contracts
* malicious deployments
* unsafe permissions

---

## Network Risk

Indicators:

* proximity to known malicious entities
* repeated interaction with flagged wallets

---

## Operational Risk

Indicators:

* compromised multisigs
* governance attacks
* suspicious admin actions

---

## Unknown Risk

Applied when Atlas detects anomalies but lacks sufficient evidence to classify them.

---

# Risk Calculation Philosophy

Risk should be calculated using multiple independent signals.

No single indicator should dominate.

Atlas combines:

```text id="z2v4jk"
Structured Rules

+

Knowledge Graph

+

Historical Memory

+

Pattern Recognition

+

Semantic Similarity

+

AI Reasoning

=

Final Risk Assessment
```

This layered approach reduces false positives.

---

# Deterministic Signals

Some indicators are rule-based.

Examples:

* interaction with known scam contract
* interaction with sanctioned wallet
* repeated bridge usage
* rapid wallet creation
* excessive transaction frequency
* repeated contract deployment

Deterministic rules are transparent and reproducible.

---

# Graph Signals

The Knowledge Graph contributes additional context.

Examples:

Wallet connected to:

Known phishing campaign

↓

Risk increases.

Wallet connected to:

Trusted exchange

↓

Risk decreases.

Relationship confidence should influence the impact of graph signals.

---

# Pattern Signals

Confirmed behavioral patterns influence risk.

Example:

Wallet matches:

Bridge Hopping Pattern

Confidence:

95%

Pattern confidence affects risk contribution.

Candidate patterns contribute less than verified patterns.

---

# Historical Signals

Historical investigations provide context.

Examples:

Repeated investigations reaching similar conclusions.

Increasing evidence over time.

Previously verified entities.

Risk should evolve as knowledge grows.

---

# Semantic Signals

Semantic similarity allows Atlas to recognize previously observed behavior.

Example:

Current investigation resembles:

Known laundering investigation.

Similarity:

94%

This similarity contributes to risk but never replaces evidence.

---

# AI Reasoning

Large language models assist in synthesizing findings into an explanation.

The AI **must not** invent risk.

The AI may:

* summarize
* explain
* prioritize
* describe uncertainty

Numerical scoring should remain grounded in structured evidence.

---

# Confidence Score

Every assessment includes confidence.

Confidence measures how reliable the assessment is.

Confidence depends on:

* evidence quantity
* evidence quality
* graph consistency
* historical agreement
* investigation completeness

High risk with low confidence should be clearly distinguished from high risk with strong evidence.

---

# Explainability

Every risk assessment should answer:

Why is this score high?

What evidence contributed?

Which relationships influenced the result?

Which patterns matched?

What uncertainty remains?

What could lower the score?

Explainability is mandatory.

---

# Risk Evolution

Risk changes over time.

Atlas tracks:

Version 1

Risk:

42

↓

Version 2

Risk:

68

↓

Version 3

Risk:

83

Every change should include:

* timestamp
* explanation
* contributing events

Users should understand why risk changed.

---

# Risk Thresholds

Atlas should avoid simplistic labels.

Instead, use investigation priorities.

Example ranges:

```text id="fq9j3w"
0–20

Minimal Concern

21–40

Low Priority

41–60

Needs Review

61–80

High Priority Investigation

81–100

Critical Investigation Required
```

These are guidance levels, not declarations of guilt.

---

# Contradictory Evidence

Evidence may both increase and decrease risk.

Example:

Wallet interacts with:

Known phishing wallet.

Risk increases.

↓

Wallet also belongs to verified exchange infrastructure.

Risk decreases.

Atlas should weigh both sides rather than ignoring one.

---

# Human Override

Future versions should allow investigators to:

* acknowledge findings
* dispute assessments
* attach notes
* provide additional evidence

Human feedback becomes additional intelligence but does not immediately overwrite automated assessments.

---

# Scheduled Reassessment

Risk should be recalculated when:

* new investigations finish
* graph relationships change
* pattern confidence changes
* new evidence appears
* sanctions lists update

Risk should never remain static if supporting knowledge evolves.

# Performance Principles

Risk calculation should:

* reuse existing intelligence
* avoid duplicate computations
* support incremental recalculation
* remain deterministic where possible
* generate explanations efficiently

---

# Failure Recovery

If AI-assisted explanation generation fails:

Atlas should still return:

* numerical assessment
* structured evidence
* deterministic explanation

LLM reasoning enhances the report but should never block risk assessment.

---

# Future Enhancements

Future versions may include:

* adaptive weighting based on investigator feedback
* graph centrality risk models
* anomaly detection models
* temporal risk forecasting
* protocol-specific scoring models
* jurisdiction-specific compliance profiles

These enhancements should integrate without replacing the explainable foundation.

---

# Guiding Principle

The Risk Engine exists to help investigators focus their attention.

It does not make accusations.

It produces transparent, evidence-backed assessments that evolve as Atlas learns more.

Every score should encourage informed investigation, not blind trust.
